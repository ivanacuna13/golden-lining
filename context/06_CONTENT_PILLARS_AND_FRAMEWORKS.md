## CHARACTER PILLARS (who you have to BE)

### C1. Work Ethic
The foundation. Parking lot to 150,000 sq ft. "Work ethic is going to be
your biggest thing." Angles: the immigrant-family operating system, outwork
as strategy, why comfort is the enemy (the pillow wall). Channel evidence:
the GC-theft survival years (2019–2021, 12–14-hour days) and "hero mode" —
the first call when things hit the fan.

### C2. Adaptability (pivot and turn)
"What worked for Mr. Beast 10-15 years ago... today he's changed the whole
model." Industries, algorithms, markets — if you can't turn quickly it
drowns you. Angles: pivots in his own career (laborer → GM → GC → owner),
reading change early.

### C3. Debt Appetite (stomach for leverage)
"The ability to hold high debt." Separates owners from employees. Angles:
debt as tool vs trap, bank relationships, how operators underwrite risk,
the closing he's working right now. Channel evidence: "tolerance to amount
of debt that you can carry and live with... that's a special talent" — and
the inverse fear of comfort making the next generation forget what made the
business great.

## TECHNICAL PILLARS (what you have to LEARN)

### T1. Sales (confident communication)
Closing deals, convincing someone to join your team, walking into a bank.
"If you're fumbling around... you're going to end up losing any
opportunities." Angles: talking with conviction, the hiring pitch, the bank
pitch.

### T2. Recruiting & Team Building
Finding people, convincing them to join, training them into owners. Proof:
regional manager → multimillionaire house builder; GM → $250-300K/yr at
Choice Corporate; another → 8-10 properties + 4 home health cares.
Angles: spotting talent, the open-book method, turning employees into owners.

### T3. Raising Capital
Bank debt, deal structuring, knowing what a lender needs to hear. (NOT
raising from the audience — institutional/bank capital for his own deals.)
Angles: how banks think, the optometrist lesson (bank > partner capital).

### T4. Tech-Forward / Computer Savvy
"Being computer savvy would be massive." Systems live in software now.
Angles: the systems he built for 12-14 properties, checklists that replaced
paperwork, running 8-9 properties without drowning.

### T5. Knowing Your Numbers
"Some of the most successful people I've met, they don't give up the books."
The 600-store / 128-Arby's / 200-Taco-Bells guy keeps the books himself.
Angles: P&L literacy, unit economics (juice bar vs bagel shop), finding what
you're bad at and hiring it, data-driven decisions. Channel-brain gap: the
old channel contains almost none of this vocabulary — "hotel" appears 10
times in 178,402 words; ADR, occupancy, revenue management, P&L, and cap
rate are effectively absent. This pillar must be created from Kush's live
knowledge, not repackaged.
