# 08 - VOICE AND TONE PROFILE (Kush Nathu)

## HOW HE SPEAKS (from the 2026-09-16 transcript)

- **Plain-spoken operator.** Short sentences, conversational, unpolished in
  the best way. Says "what do you call it" as a filler — editors clean it.
- **Story-first.** Every principle arrives inside a story: the optometrist,
  the juice bar, the toilet paper, the 600-store guy. Never lead with the
  abstraction — lead with the story, land the principle.
- **Analogies from real life.** Avengers recruiting ("Hulk smashes things"),
  the wall vs the pillow, Mr. Beast's pivot. He reaches for vivid pictures.
- **Open book.** "I give you the bad and I give you the good." Admits debt,
  headaches, failures. This is the trust engine — never polish it away.
- **Karma and giving** are sincere, not branding. He genuinely lights up
  describing people he helped who now provide for their families.
- **Confident without performing.** No hype voice, no guru cadence. Believes
  what he says because he lived it — "I believe in it. I trust in what I do."
- **Interviewer instinct (channel-proven).** On the old channel he gets real
  operators talking on their turf. Keep the instinct; change the subject —
  the subject becomes Kush's own world.
- **"Hero mode" language.** He speaks in first-call-when-it-hits-the-fan
  terms; crisis is where he's clearest. Prompts should aim him at those
  moments.

## TONE RULES

- Warm but direct. Like a mentor who's seen you fail before you do.
- Never preachy, never motivational-poster. If a line could go on a poster,
  cut it or ground it in a story.
- Numbers and specifics beat adjectives. "150,000 square feet" > "massive."
- Keep his cultural texture (Arlington, Hispanic community upbringing,
  family business) — it's differentiation, not decoration.
- Quarantine the old channel's generic motivational voice — the
  biography-shorts cadence must never leak into the new scripts.

## RECORDING METHOD

**Prompts, NOT scripts.** He knows the material cold; word-for-word scripts
water him down. Filming prompts look like:

> "Break down the Triangle — the three places confident communication wins
> or loses you the deal: closing, hiring, and the bank. Tell the story of
> [X]. Land on: fumbling costs you the opportunity."

His stated concern: rambling, repetition, pauses. That's the editor's job,
not his. Film long, cut tight. Target ~10 minutes to start; let retention
data set the length. His best old-channel moments are when he stops
interviewing and talks shop (GC theft, hero mode, debt) — prompts should aim
him at those moments.
